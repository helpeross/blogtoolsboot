# GaN Chargers Explained: Why You Should Upgrade Your Charging Setup in 2026

If your laptop brick takes up two outlet spaces and your phone charger lives in a drawer, it's time to talk about GaN.

Gallium nitride (GaN) chargers replaced the older silicon-based chargers in almost every category worth buying. They run cooler, shrink to a fraction of the size, and support the fast-charging standards modern devices expect.

## What makes GaN different?

Traditional chargers use **silicon** semiconductors, which have been around for decades. **Gallium nitride** is a newer semiconductor material that:

- Switches power far more efficiently → **less heat**
- Operates at higher frequencies → **smaller components**
- Delivers the same power in **half the size** (or less)

The practical result: a 65W GaN charger fits in your palm, while a 65W silicon charger is roughly the size of a deck of cards stacked tall. And because they run cooler, they also age better.

## How much wattage do you actually need?

The most common mistake is buying too small — or paying extra for power you never use. Here's a simple rule of thumb:

| Use case | Minimum wattage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Phone only | 20–30W | Fast charging for most modern phones |
| Phone + tablet | 30–45W | Comfortable headroom for two devices |
| Ultrabook / thin-and-light laptop | 45–65W | USB-C PD is standard on modern laptops |
| Powerful 16" laptop | 100–140W | Check your laptop's USB-C PD spec first |
| Laptop + phone + accessories | 100W+ | Multi-device travelers |

> **Important:** before going smaller, check that your laptop actually charges over USB-C. Many business laptops require 65W or higher over USB-C Power Delivery — the charger label must match or exceed your laptop's requirement.

## Ports: the layout question

Two-port vs. multi-port matters more than you'd think:

- **1 × USB-C + 1 × USB-A** — the classic combo. Good for a phone plus a legacy cable.
- **2 × USB-C** — ideal if you charge two USB-C devices (e.g. laptop + phone).
- **3+ ports** — convenient, but check the "output distribution" table. Many chargers split wattage when multiple ports are in use (e.g. 65W total = 40W + 25W). Make sure the split still covers your laptop's needs.

## Safety and standards to look for

- **USB Power Delivery (PD) 3.0 / 3.1** — the standard fast-charging protocol for laptops and most modern phones.
- **PPS support** — needed for some phones' fastest charging modes (notably Samsung and Google).
- **Certifications** — look for UL, CE, FCC, or your region's safety marks. A cheap unbranded 100W charger is a fire risk, not a bargain.
- **Grounded plug** — for 100W+ chargers, a grounded (3-prong) plug reduces electrical noise and is safer with certain laptops.

## Our recommendation strategy

Our buying advice, consistent across our reviews:

1. **Pick wattage by your laptop**, not your phone — the laptop is the ceiling.
2. **Buy one charger for travel** (65–100W, 2–3 ports) and keep the stock brick at home.
3. **Spend a little more on a known brand** in the 30–65W range; the price gap is tiny and the safety gap is real.
4. **Avoid super-cheap high-wattage chargers** with no certifications.
